Headline decoder

Stop nodding along.

News is written for people who already know the words. Here’s what this week’s headlines actually say.

The headline

“Anthropic targets a $2 trillion valuation in its IPO”

What it means

Anthropic wants to sell shares to anyone, at a price that makes the whole company worth $2 trillion.

The ideas inside

IPO · Valuation · Market cap

The headline

“The Fed holds the fed funds rate at 3.75%–4%”

What it means

America’s central bank kept the rate banks charge each other the same. Two officials wanted it higher.

The ideas inside

The Fed · Fed funds rate · Inflation

The headline

“Mortgage rates top 7% as the 10-year Treasury yield hits its highest since 2002”

What it means

Home loans cost over 7% a year again. They follow what the US government pays to borrow for 10 years.

The ideas inside

Mortgage rate · Treasury · Yield

The headline

“Starbucks US comparable sales jump 7.7%”

What it means

Its US cafes open over a year sold 7.7% more. Most of it came from people spending more per visit.

The ideas inside

Comparable sales · Average ticket

The headline

“Big Tech’s capex doubles to $202 billion”

What it means

Amazon, Google, Meta and Microsoft spent $202 billion on buildings and equipment in three months, mostly for AI. That’s twice last year.

The ideas inside

Capex · Free cash flow

Not just for finance people

The news is about your money.

Most headlines reach your wallet. You just can’t see how until someone explains it.

🏠

Your home loan

Mortgage rates follow a government bond. In late October they hit 7.24%, up from 6.17% a year before. On a $400,000 loan, that’s $2,726 a month instead of $2,442.

⛽

Your gas

A war near Iran pushed oil up 76% since February. A gallon of regular gas went from $3.04 to $4.27 in a year.

🏢

Your job

The company you work for is in the news too. Learn the ideas and you’ll see how it makes money, and why its profit moved last quarter.

Questions

Good questions.

What is a daily story?

One real piece of money or business news, explained in about 30 swipe cards. Every idea in it is taught from zero. You get a simple example first, then the real numbers, a chart or two and a quick check.

How deep does it go?

As deep as you want. Every idea has a layer under it that explains it. Swipe up to open the next layer. Swipe back to return to the story.

Where do the numbers come from?

Company filings, official data like the Federal Reserve and the Labor Department, and news reports. Every fact card shows its source. Green tags mean a filing or official number. Gold tags mean a news report.

Who is Finspeak for?

Anyone who reads money news and feels lost when the jargon starts. Nothing is assumed. If a word stops you, swipe up and it gets explained, all the way down.

Is this investment advice?

No. Finspeak explains what the news and the words mean. When people disagree, you see both sides. It never tells you what to buy or sell.
